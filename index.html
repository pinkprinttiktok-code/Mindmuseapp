        What's actually on it

        • SATDigital and adaptive since 2024. Two sections — Reading & Writing, and Math — scored 200–800 each. Shorter passages than the old paper test.
        • ACTEnglish, Math, Reading, Science, each scored 1–36 and averaged. Faster paced. Students who read quickly often prefer it.
        • Take both onceSit a free practice version of each before committing. Most students score meaningfully better on one, and there's no way to predict which.

        Free prep that actually works

        • Official practice testsCollege Board's Bluebook app and ACT's official materials. Real retired questions beat any third-party imitation.
        • Khan AcademyFree, official College Board partner, and it adapts to your practice test results.
        • Your error logThe highest-leverage free tool there is. Write down every miss and why. Patterns show up within two tests.

        The part nobody tells you

        Test-optional is now the norm at a large share of colleges, and at many of them a strong GPA with no score submitted beats a mediocre score submitted. Before you spend six months on a 60-point gain, check whether the schools on your list even require it — and if they don't, whether your score is above or below their published middle range. Below it, sending nothing is often the stronger application.

        What moves a GPA

        • Earlier is worth moreA freshman semester changes your average far more than a senior one, because each new semester is a smaller share of the total.
        • Grade replacementMany schools let you retake a failed or low course and replace the grade. Ask your counselor — it's rarely advertised.
        • Weighted vs unweightedMost colleges recalculate your GPA their own way, usually stripping weights. Chasing weighted points by overloading on APs can backfire.

        Before you overload

        • Trajectory countsAdmissions readers look at the shape of your transcript. A rising line from sophomore to senior year reads well even if the average is modest.
        • Course rigor is judged in contextYou're compared against what your school offers, not a national list. Four APs at a school offering six is stronger than four at a school offering thirty.
        • One extra AP can cost two gradesStacking a fifth AP that drops two B's into C's is a net loss on every measure.

        The part nobody tells you

        If the calculator tells you your target is out of reach, that is useful information and not a verdict on you. Community college transfer pathways send thousands of students into flagship universities every year with the same degree at the end and a fraction of the debt. Several states guarantee admission to their flagship for transfer students who hit a GPA threshold. Knowing this as a junior lets you plan a route instead of grieving one.

        The part nobody tells you

        Rereading notes and highlighting feel productive and are close to useless, because recognizing something is not the same as being able to retrieve it. The two techniques with the strongest evidence behind them are testing yourself and spacing that testing out over time, which is exactly what a flashcard deck plus a review schedule does. If you only change one thing about how you study, close the book and try to write down what was on the page. The discomfort of that is the learning.
